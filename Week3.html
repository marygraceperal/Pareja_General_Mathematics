Learning Intentions

Learning Objectives

At the end of the five-session learning sequence, learners should be able to:

  1. Identify the essential parts of a cash flow stream involving an annuity, including payment, interest rate, payment frequency, and number of payments.
  2. Explain why the fair market value of a future cash flow stream is determined by its present value.
  3. Calculate the present value or fair market value of an ordinary annuity and a cash flow stream containing an annuity.
  4. Interpret the calculated fair market value in practical Philippine financial situations and justify a reasonable financial decision.

Learner Context and Planning Notes

  • Target learners are average-level Grade 11 students.
  • Use familiar contexts such as installment payments, savings, tuition-related payments, household purchases, and small-business cash flows.
  • Begin with simple numerical values before introducing more complex cash-flow streams.
  • Emphasize the meaning of the answer, not only mechanical substitution into a formula.
  • Allow calculator or spreadsheet use after learners understand the mathematical structure.

Fact Sheet / Student Study Notes

Read, understand, then practice.

What You Will Learn

In this lesson, you will learn how to determine the fair market value of a future cash flow stream when part of that stream consists of an annuity.

  • Recognize a cash flow stream.
  • Understand fair market value as a present-value concept.
  • Calculate the present value of an ordinary annuity.
  • Calculate the present value of separate future cash flows.
  • Combine present values to determine fair market value.
  • Interpret the answer using Philippine peso values.

1. Essential Definitions

Cash flow: Money received or paid at a particular point in time.
Cash flow stream: A sequence of cash inflows or outflows occurring at different times.
Present value: The value today of a future amount or series of future amounts, considering an interest rate.
Fair market value: In this lesson, the present value of the future cash flows represented by the cash flow stream.
Annuity: A sequence of equal payments made at equal time intervals.
Ordinary annuity: An annuity in which payments are made at the end of each period.
Periodic interest rate: The interest rate that applies to one payment period.

2. Key Ideas and Formulas

Present Value of a Single Future Amount

PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ
  • PV = present value
  • FV = future value
  • i = interest rate per period
  • n = number of periods

Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ] ÷ i
  • PMT = equal periodic payment
  • i = interest rate per payment period
  • n = total number of payments

Fair Market Value of a Cash Flow Stream

FMV = PV of annuity component + PV of each separate cash flow
Important: The interest rate and payment period must match. If payments are monthly, use the monthly rate and the total number of monthly payments.

3. Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1 — Present Value of One Future Amount

You will receive ₱10,000 two years from now. The annual interest rate is 5%. Find its present value.

Step 1: Identify values: FV = ₱10,000, i = 0.05, n = 2.
Step 2: Use PV = FV ÷ (1 + i)ⁿ.
Step 3: PV = ₱10,000 ÷ (1.05)².
Step 4: PV = ₱10,000 ÷ 1.1025.
Answer: PV ≈ ₱9,070.29.

Example 2 — Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

A student will receive ₱3,000 at the end of each year for 3 years. The discount rate is 6% per year. Find the present value.

Step 1: PMT = ₱3,000, i = 0.06, n = 3.
Step 2: PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ] ÷ i.
Step 3: PV = ₱3,000 × [1 − (1.06)⁻³] ÷ 0.06.
Step 4: (1.06)⁻³ ≈ 0.839619.
Step 5: PV ≈ ₱3,000 × 2.673012.
Answer: PV ≈ ₱8,019.04.

Example 3 — Monthly Annuity

A cash flow provides ₱2,500 at the end of every month for 12 months. The annual rate is 12%, compounded monthly. Find the present value.

Step 1: PMT = ₱2,500.
Step 2: Monthly rate: i = 0.12 ÷ 12 = 0.01.
Step 3: Number of payments: n = 12.
Step 4: PV = ₱2,500 × [1 − (1.01)⁻¹²] ÷ 0.01.
Step 5: (1.01)⁻¹² ≈ 0.887449.
Step 6: PV ≈ ₱2,500 × 11.255077.
Answer: PV ≈ ₱28,137.69.

Example 4 — Cash Flow Stream Containing an Annuity

An investor will receive ₱4,000 at the end of each year for 3 years, plus an additional ₱10,000 at the end of Year 3. The annual discount rate is 8%. Find the fair market value today.

Step 1: Find the present value of the ₱4,000 annuity.
Step 2: PV annuity = ₱4,000 × [1 − (1.08)⁻³] ÷ 0.08.
Step 3: PV annuity ≈ ₱10,308.67.
Step 4: Find the present value of ₱10,000 received at Year 3.
Step 5: PV lump sum = ₱10,000 ÷ (1.08)³ ≈ ₱7,938.32.
Step 6: FMV = ₱10,308.67 + ₱7,938.32.
Answer: FMV ≈ ₱18,246.99.

4. A Reliable Problem-Solving Routine

  1. Draw the timeline.
  2. Identify the payment amount.
  3. Identify payment frequency.
  4. Convert the interest rate to the payment period.
  5. Determine the number of periods.
  6. Choose the correct present-value formula.
  7. Calculate each component.
  8. Add the present values.
  9. Interpret the result.

5. Common Mistakes and Pitfalls

Mistake Why It Is Wrong Correct Practice
Using 12% directly for monthly payments The rate does not match the monthly period. Use 12% ÷ 12 = 1% per month.
Using years when payments are monthly The number of periods must match the rate period. Convert years into total months.
Adding future cash flows without discounting Future money is not valued at today's amount. Find each cash flow's present value.
Forgetting the separate lump sum The annuity formula covers only the equal recurring payments. Calculate the lump sum separately.
Rounding too early Early rounding can change the final answer. Keep several decimal places until the final answer.

Teacher Notes

Implementation, remediation and enrichment guide

Teaching Advice

  • Always begin with the timeline before presenting the formula.
  • Ask learners to identify the payment period before calculating anything.
  • Write the rate conversion explicitly, especially for monthly payments.
  • Use ₱ consistently to reinforce the Philippine context.
  • Do not allow calculators to hide conceptual errors.
  • After calculation, ask: “What does this amount mean today?”

Common Learner Misconceptions

Misconception Teacher Response
“12% is always the value of i.” Ask: “What is one payment period?” Then convert the rate.
“Two years always means n = 2.” Ask whether payments are annual, quarterly, or monthly.
“Fair market value is just the sum of future payments.” Use the timeline to show that future amounts must be discounted.
“The annuity formula includes every cash flow.” Separate equal recurring payments from additional lump sums.
“The calculator answer is automatically correct.” Require learners to show the formula and identify inputs first.

Suggested Remediation

  1. Return to identifying PMT, i, and n without calculating.
  2. Use a simple three-period timeline.
  3. Practice converting annual rates to monthly rates.
  4. Use single future amounts before returning to annuities.
  5. Use partially completed solutions where learners fill missing steps.
  6. Move from one annuity to an annuity plus one lump sum.

Enrichment

Advanced learners may compare two different cash-flow offers, calculate their present values, and make a written recommendation supported by mathematical evidence.

Extension Question: How would the fair market value change if the discount rate increased? Ask learners to make a prediction before recalculating.
