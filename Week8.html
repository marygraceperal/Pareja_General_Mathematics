Weekly Learning Intentions

  • Interpret a stated scale and use it to determine real distances from map, blueprint, or sketch measurements.
  • Compare distances represented using the same scale and explain which real distance is greater.
  • Convert amounts between currencies using a stated exchange rate.
  • Convert time units and solve practical elapsed-time, schedule, and travel problems.
Evidence-informed cycle: retrieve prior unit-conversion knowledge → represent the situation visually → explicitly model the relationship → guided practice → independent application → analyze errors → provide targeted remediation or extension.

SESSION 1 — Scale on Maps, Blueprints, and Sketches

Daily objective: Interpret a scale and calculate real distances from map, blueprint, and sketch measurements.

ACTIVITY

Elicit

Show a simple map and a room sketch. Ask: “If 1 cm on the drawing represents 2 m in reality, what does 5 cm represent?” Learners estimate before calculating.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Compare scale statements such as 1 cm : 2 m and 1 cm : 5 m. Discuss what the scale means and why the same drawing measurement can represent different real distances under different scales.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Write the scale as a ratio or statement. Keep units consistent, then multiply or divide according to the known-to-unknown relationship.

Example: 1 cm represents 2 m.
6 cm × 2 m/cm = 12 m.

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Solve map, blueprint, and sketch problems. Learners record drawing measurement, scale, calculation, and real distance. Compare two represented routes or rooms.

Evidence-to-action: If learners reverse the scale, use a two-column “drawing distance → real distance” organizer. If units are mixed, require unit matching before calculation.

SESSION 2 — Currency Conversion

Daily objective: Convert amounts between currencies using a stated exchange rate and interpret the result in a real-life transaction.

ACTIVITY

Connect

Present a travel-budget scenario. For example, if ₱1 = US$0.017, estimate how many US dollars ₱10,000 would represent.

ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Compare multiplying and dividing by an exchange rate. Ask which operation produces an answer that is sensible for the direction of conversion. Emphasize that the stated rate determines the operation.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Use a conversion factor with units so the original currency cancels.

If ₱1 = US$0.017:
₱10,000 × (US$0.017/₱1) = US$170.

Exchange rates can change; use the rate supplied in the problem.

APPLICATION

Practice

Convert Philippine pesos to US dollars and US dollars to Philippine pesos using stated rates. Apply the conversions to travel budgets and purchasing scenarios.

Teacher response: When learners reverse the rate, have them write the conversion factor with currency units and check whether the original currency cancels.

SESSION 3 — Time Conversions and Real-World Schedules

Daily objective: Convert between common time units and solve elapsed-time and schedule problems in real-world contexts.

ACTIVITY

Retrieve

Quick conversion relay: seconds ↔ minutes, minutes ↔ hours, hours ↔ days. Then read a short travel or school schedule and identify start time, end time, and duration.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Compare 2.5 hours with 2 hours 30 minutes. Discuss why decimal hours and clock notation are not interchangeable without conversion.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
1 minute = 60 seconds
1 hour = 60 minutes
1 day = 24 hours
Elapsed time = end time − start time, accounting for crossing hour/day boundaries.

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Solve travel duration, work/project schedule, daily routine, and transaction-duration problems. Convert decimal hours into hours and minutes where required.

Evidence-to-action: If learners struggle across midnight, draw a timeline. If decimal-hour conversion is difficult, separate the whole-hour and fractional-hour parts.

SESSION 4 — Integrated Scale, Currency, and Time Problems

Daily objective: Select and apply an appropriate conversion strategy to integrated real-world problems involving scale, currency, and time.

ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Formula/conversion-factor sort: learners classify situations as scale, currency, or time and choose the needed relationship before calculating.

ANALYSIS

Reasoning

Analyze incorrect solutions and identify whether the error is in reading the scale, direction of currency conversion, unit conversion, or elapsed-time reasoning.

ABSTRACTION

Synthesis

Use the routine: Understand → Represent → Choose relationship → Calculate → Check units → Interpret.

A correct numerical answer is not enough: the unit and context must also make sense.

APPLICATION

Mastery

Complete a mini travel-planning task combining map distance, currency budget, and travel duration. Learners justify each conversion and communicate the final decision.

Evidence-to-action: Use the mastery evidence to identify the exact failed process. Reteach only the needed step, then provide a parallel problem for reassessment.

LEARNER GUIDE

Scale, Currency, and Time Conversions

A practical guide for interpreting measurements and making real-world conversion decisions.

What You Will Learn

  • How to interpret scales on maps, blueprints, and sketches.
  • How to compare real distances represented by drawings.
  • How to convert currencies using a stated exchange rate.
  • How to convert time units and calculate elapsed time.
  • How to check whether a conversion result makes sense.

Essential Definitions

TermMeaning
ScaleA relationship between a measurement on a drawing or map and the corresponding real measurement.
Map distanceThe measured distance between locations on a map.
Real distanceThe actual distance represented by a map, blueprint, or sketch.
Exchange rateThe stated value relationship used to convert one currency into another.
Conversion factorA ratio equal to 1 that allows a quantity to be expressed in another unit.
Elapsed timeThe amount of time between a starting event and an ending event.

Key Relationships

SituationUseful relationship
ScaleReal distance = drawing distance × real-distance-per-drawing-unit, when the scale is stated that way.
CurrencyConverted amount = original amount × stated exchange-rate factor.
Time1 min = 60 s; 1 h = 60 min; 1 day = 24 h.
Elapsed timeEnd time − start time, using a timeline when the interval crosses an hour or day boundary.

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

1. Scale

A map uses the scale 1 cm represents 4 km. Two towns are 7 cm apart on the map.

Real distance = 7 cm × 4 km/cm = 28 km.

2. Compare Scale Distances

On the same map, Route A measures 5 cm and Route B measures 8 cm. The scale is 1 cm : 4 km.

A = 5×4 = 20 km.
B = 8×4 = 32 km.
Difference = 32 − 20 = 12 km. Route B is longer.

3. Currency

Suppose the stated rate is ₱1 = US$0.017. Convert ₱10,000 to US dollars.

₱10,000 × US$0.017/₱1 = US$170.

4. Reverse Currency Conversion

Using the same stated rate, convert US$255 to pesos.

US$255 ÷ US$0.017 per ₱1 = ₱15,000.

5. Time

A trip starts at 8:35 AM and ends at 11:20 AM.

8:35 → 10:35 = 2 h; 10:35 → 11:20 = 45 min. Total = 2 h 45 min.

6. Decimal Hours

A project takes 2.5 hours.

2.5 h = 2 h + 0.5 h. Since 0.5×60 = 30, the duration is 2 h 30 min.

Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Reversing the scale relationship.
  • Mixing centimetres, metres, and kilometres without converting or using a consistent scale.
  • Using an exchange rate in the wrong direction.
  • Assuming a currency rate is universal instead of using the rate stated in the problem.
  • Treating 2.5 hours as 2 hours 5 minutes instead of 2 hours 30 minutes.
  • Forgetting that time intervals can cross noon, midnight, or a date boundary.

Real-World Connections

Scale is used in maps, building plans, floor plans, engineering drawings, and sketches. Currency conversion is useful in travel, online purchasing, budgeting, and international transactions. Time conversion is used in travel planning, project management, work schedules, school routines, and business transactions.

Activity Sheet — Complete Solutions

  1. 4 cm × 5 km/cm = 20 km.
  2. 7 cm is longer. Under the same scale, the larger map measurement represents the larger real distance.
  3. ₱1,000 × US$0.02/₱1 = US$20.
  4. 2×60 = 120 minutes.
  5. 180÷60 = 3 minutes.
  6. 2.5 h = 2 h + 0.5 h; 0.5×60 = 30 min. Answer: 2 h 30 min.
  7. 6.5 cm × 2 m/cm = 13 m.
  8. A = 5×4 = 20 km. B = 8×4 = 32 km. Difference = 12 km.
  9. ₱12,000 × US$0.017/₱1 = US$204.
  10. US$204 ÷ US$0.017 per ₱1 = ₱12,000.
  11. 7:45 → 10:30 = 2 h 45 min.
  12. 3.75 h = 3 h + 0.75 h; 0.75×60 = 45 min. Answer: 3 h 45 min.
  13. The learner reversed the scale relationship. 10 cm × 5 km/cm = 50 km, not 2 km.
  14. Dividing ₱10,000 by 0.017 is incorrect for this stated direction. ₱10,000 × US$0.017/₱1 = US$170.
  15. 11:40 PM → midnight = 20 min; midnight → 1:15 AM = 1 h 15 min. Total = 1 h 35 min.
  16. Length = 8×1.5 = 12 m. Width = 6×1.5 = 9 m.
  17. ₱25,000 × US$0.017/₱1 = US$425. Since US$425 ≥ US$400, the stated amount is sufficient for the US$400 item, leaving US$25, before considering any other costs or fees.
  18. Answers vary. A valid response must state a scale and map measurement, an exchange rate and conversion, a travel duration, correct calculations with units, and a reasonable interpretation.

  2. B. US$160. ₱8,000×0.02 = US$160.

TEACHER NOTES

Support, Remediation and Enrichment

Common Learner Misconceptions

  • Reading a scale backward.
  • Failing to maintain consistent units in scale problems.
  • Multiplying or dividing by an exchange rate without checking its stated direction.
  • Assuming a sample exchange rate is a current market rate rather than the rate supplied by the problem.
  • Confusing decimal hours with hours-and-minutes notation.
  • Failing to account for crossing midnight.

Remediation

  • Use a two-column conversion table for map and real measurements.
  • Write currency conversion factors with units before calculating.
  • Use number lines/timelines for elapsed-time problems.
  • Begin with one-step conversions before integrated problems.
  • Use error-analysis cards: identify the exact step where the solution went wrong.
  • Require learners to state the unit and meaning of the final answer.

Enrichment

  • Design a scaled floor plan of a familiar room.
  • Create a travel budget using a teacher-provided exchange rate and compare two destinations.
  • Plan a one-day itinerary and calculate total travel and activity time.
  • Create a spreadsheet that automatically converts scale distances, currencies, or time units.
