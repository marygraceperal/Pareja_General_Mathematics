SESSION 1 — Choosing Appropriate Metric Units

Daily objective: Determine an appropriate metric unit for a given quantity and justify the choice using the size and context of the measurement.

ACTIVITY

Elicit

Show contexts such as the length of a pencil, mass of a rice sack, amount of medicine, distance between cities, and capacity of a water container. Learners choose likely units: mm, cm, m, km, mg, g, kg, mL, or L.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Sort choices into small, medium, and large quantities. Discuss why 5 km is sensible for a road distance but 5 mm is not. Identify the clue words and scale of each situation.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

The metric system uses prefixes to express powers of ten. Common length units include millimetre, centimetre, metre, and kilometre; mass includes milligram, gram, and kilogram; capacity includes millilitre and litre.

kilo = 1,000 units • centi = 0.01 unit • milli = 0.001 unit

APPLICATION

Independent

Choose the most appropriate unit for 10 classroom objects or situations. Exit ticket: select a unit for the width of a phone, mass of a school bag, and distance from school to home, then justify each.

Evidence-to-action: If learners choose units without considering scale, use an actual ruler or familiar object as a reference point and ask them to estimate before measuring.

SESSION 2 — Converting Within the Metric System

Daily objective: Convert measurements within the metric system accurately by using powers-of-ten relationships and verify whether the answer is reasonable.

ACTIVITY

Connect

Use a metric ladder: km → hm → dam → m → dm → cm → mm. Learners predict what happens to the numerical value when moving toward smaller or larger units.

ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Compare 3.5 m = 350 cm with an incorrect claim of 0.35 cm. Ask: Did the unit become smaller? Should the number become larger or smaller?

ABSTRACTION

Scaffold

Within a metric system, conversion factors are powers of ten. Moving from a larger unit to a smaller unit generally increases the numerical value; moving from a smaller unit to a larger unit decreases it.

3.5 m × 100 cm/m = 350 cm

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Convert length, mass, and capacity measures. Learners write the conversion factor with units so that the original unit cancels.

Teacher response: When learners reverse a conversion, require them to estimate first: converting to a smaller unit should usually produce a larger numerical count of units.

SESSION 3 — Converting Between Measurement Systems

Daily objective: Convert measurements between different systems using an appropriate stated conversion factor and preserve the meaning of the quantity.

ACTIVITY

Retrieve

Review dimensional conversion with familiar examples: 1 inch = 2.54 cm and 1 mile ≈ 1.609 km. Learners decide which factor will cancel the starting unit.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Analyze why 10 in × 2.54 cm/in gives centimetres. Compare with an incorrectly oriented factor and identify the unit that fails to cancel.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Use conversion factors as equivalent ratios equal to 1. Arrange them so the unwanted unit cancels.

10 in × 2.54 cm/1 in = 25.4 cm

For this week's classroom problems, use the conversion relationship provided in the item.

APPLICATION

Practice

Convert inches to centimetres, feet to metres using 1 ft = 0.3048 m, miles to kilometres using the stated approximation, and pounds to kilograms using 1 lb ≈ 0.4536 kg.

Ways forward: If learners struggle with factor orientation, make “wanted unit on top” the first scaffold, then fade the prompt as accuracy improves.

SESSION 4 — Real-Life Measurement Conversion Problems

Daily objective: Solve multi-step real-life conversion problems, show the conversion factors used, and interpret the final measurement with correct units.

ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Formula-free conversion sprint: identify the starting unit, desired unit, and likely conversion factor for five scenarios.

ANALYSIS

Reasoning

Annotate a word problem: quantity → starting unit → desired unit → conversion factor → computation → reasonableness check. Compare two solution paths.

ABSTRACTION

Synthesis
Read → identify units → select factor → arrange factor to cancel → multiply/divide → write final unit → estimate/check.

Correct units are part of the mathematical answer, not decoration.

APPLICATION

Mastery

Independently solve classroom, cooking, travel, science, and measurement scenarios. Learners must show the conversion factor and explain why the final value is reasonable.

Evidence-to-action: Classify learners as Not Yet Mastered, Developing, or Mastered. Reteach the exact failed step—unit selection, factor orientation, arithmetic, or interpretation—before reassessment.

LEARNER GUIDE

Metric Units and Measurement Conversions

Study guide for selecting and converting measurement units.

What You Will Learn

  • How to choose an appropriate unit for a measurement.
  • How to convert within the metric system.
  • How to convert between measurement systems using stated conversion factors.
  • How to solve real-life conversion problems and check reasonableness.

Essential Definitions

TermMeaning
Metric unitA unit from the metric system, such as metre, gram, or litre.
ConversionThe process of expressing the same quantity in a different unit.
Conversion factorAn equivalent ratio used to change units without changing the quantity.
Dimensional analysisA method that uses units to guide calculations and check whether the result has the desired unit.
Reasonableness checkEstimating or inspecting the result to determine whether it makes sense in context.

Metric Relationships

PrefixMeaningExample
kilo1,0001 km = 1,000 m
centi1/1001 m = 100 cm
milli1/1,0001 m = 1,000 mm
1 km = 1,000 m = 100,000 cm = 1,000,000 mm

Key Conversion Method

Use a conversion factor so the starting unit cancels.

3.2 m × 100 cm/1 m = 320 cm

The unit m appears once on top and once on the bottom, so it cancels. The desired unit, cm, remains.

Worked Examples

1. Choosing a Unit

What is a reasonable unit for the distance between Butuan City and another city several hundred kilometres away?

Kilometre (km) is appropriate because the distance is large.

2. Metric Conversion

Convert 4.5 m to centimetres.

4.5 m × 100 cm/1 m = 450 cm.

3. Cross-System Conversion

Convert 12 inches to centimetres using 1 in = 2.54 cm.

12 in × 2.54 cm/1 in = 30.48 cm.

4. Real-Life Application

A recipe requires 750 mL of water. Express this in litres.

750 mL × 1 L/1,000 mL = 0.75 L.

5. Multi-Step Conversion

A runner travels 5 miles. Using 1 mile ≈ 1.609 km, estimate the distance in kilometres.

5 × 1.609 = 8.045 km, or about 8.05 km.

Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Choosing a unit without considering the size of the quantity.
  • Moving the decimal in the wrong direction.
  • Using a conversion factor upside down.
  • Dropping the unit during computation.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Failing to check whether the result is reasonable.

Real-World Connections

Measurement conversions are used in cooking, medicine, science, engineering, construction, travel, sports, shopping, and everyday estimation. A correct numerical answer without a correct unit is incomplete.

Quick Self-Check

  1. Which unit is appropriate for the length of a pencil?
  2. Convert 2.5 m to cm.
  3. Convert 300 cm to m.
  4. Using 1 in = 2.54 cm, convert 8 in to cm.
  5. Why should the unwanted unit cancel when using a conversion factor?

Activity Sheet — Solutions

  1. cm is a reasonable unit for pencil length.
  2. kg is a reasonable unit for the mass of a school bag.
  3. 1 m = 100 cm.
  4. 1 cm = 10 mm.
  5. 3.5 m × 100 cm/1 m = 350 cm.
  6. 2,400 mL × 1 L/1,000 mL = 2.4 L.
  7. 4.2 km × 1,000 m/1 km = 4,200 m.
  8. 650 cm × 1 m/100 cm = 6.5 m.
  9. 7,500 mg × 1 g/1,000 mg = 7.5 g.
  10. 12 in × 2.54 cm/1 in = 30.48 cm.
  11. 5 ft × 0.3048 m/1 ft = 1.524 m.
  12. 2.5 L = 2,500 mL. 2,500 ÷ 500 = 5 full bottles.
  13. The learner moved the decimal in the wrong direction. 3 m × 100 cm/1 m = 300 cm.
  14. 8.5 km × 1,000 m/km = 8,500 m; 8,500 m × 100 cm/m = 850,000 cm.
  15. 6 miles × 1.609 km/mile = 9.654 km, approximately 9.65 km.
  16. 1.25 L × 1,000 mL/L = 1,250 mL. The numerical value increases because a litre contains 1,000 smaller millilitres.
  17. 0.075 kg × 1,000 g/kg = 75 g; 75 g × 1,000 mg/g = 75,000 mg.
  18. Answers vary. A valid response must contain a realistic situation, two valid conversion factors, correct factor orientation, final unit, and accurate computation.

Assessment — Solutions

  2. 4.5 m × 100 cm/m = 450 cm.

TEACHER NOTES

Support, Remediation and Enrichment

Common Misconceptions

  • Choosing units by familiarity rather than scale.
  • Moving the decimal in the wrong direction.
  • Multiplying or dividing without identifying the unit relationship.
  • Using a conversion factor upside down.
  • Dropping or changing units during a multi-step calculation.

Remediation

  • Begin with concrete objects and estimates before symbolic conversion.
  • Use a metric ladder for same-system conversions.
  • Require learners to circle the starting unit and underline the desired unit.
  • Teach “write the desired unit in the numerator” as an initial scaffold for dimensional analysis.
  • Require a reasonableness statement after each real-life problem.

Enrichment

  • Compare the same measurement expressed in three metric units.
  • Create a classroom measurement-conversion chart.
  • Use a spreadsheet to automate repeated conversions and investigate rounding.
  • Design a mini “unit detective” activity in which learners identify and correct conversion errors.
