Weekly Learning Intentions

  • Identify and distinguish Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin temperature scales.
  • Convert temperatures accurately between Celsius and Fahrenheit.
  • Convert temperatures between Celsius and Kelvin and interpret the result.
  • Select and apply an appropriate temperature conversion in real-world situations.
  • Check whether a converted temperature is reasonable in its context.
Evidence-informed cycle: retrieve prior knowledge → represent the temperature situation → explicitly model the conversion relationship → guided practice → independent practice → error analysis → targeted remediation or enrichment.

SESSION 1 — Celsius and Fahrenheit

Daily objective: Convert temperatures between Celsius and Fahrenheit using the appropriate formula and interpret the result.

ACTIVITY

Elicit

Present familiar temperatures such as a cool room, a hot day, and boiling water. Ask learners which scale they have encountered and whether the numerical values would be the same.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Compare the formulas and discuss why 0°C does not equal 0°F. Identify the roles of multiplication, division, and the constant 32.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
°F = (9/5)(°C) + 32
°C = (5/9)(°F − 32)

Keep the temperature unit visible throughout the solution.

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Convert selected weather and household temperatures. Learners estimate first, calculate second, and explain whether the answer is reasonable.

Evidence-to-action: If learners forget the −32 step when converting to Celsius, use a formula map showing the operation sequence.

SESSION 2 — Celsius and Kelvin

Daily objective: Convert temperatures between Celsius and Kelvin and explain the relationship between the two scales.

ACTIVITY

Connect

Discuss temperatures in a scientific setting. Ask why a laboratory may use Kelvin instead of Celsius.

ANALYSIS

Reasoning

Compare equal temperature intervals on Celsius and Kelvin. Identify the fixed numerical difference between the two scales.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
K = °C + 273.15
°C = K − 273.15

Kelvin is written as K, without a degree symbol.

APPLICATION

Practice

Convert temperatures associated with scientific experiments and storage conditions. Explain what each final value means.

Teacher response: When learners add or subtract 273.15 in the wrong direction, require them to state the starting scale and target scale before calculating.

SESSION 3 — Temperature in Real-World Contexts

Daily objective: Apply temperature conversions to weather, recipes, storage, and scientific scenarios.

ACTIVITY

Retrieve

Quick conversion cards: learners identify the source and target scales, choose the formula, and estimate the direction of the answer.

ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Inspect incorrect solutions and locate whether the error occurred in formula selection, substitution, arithmetic, or interpretation.

ABSTRACTION

Model

Use the routine: Identify scale → Identify target → Select formula → Substitute → Calculate → Label → Interpret → Check.

APPLICATION

Transfer

Solve scenarios involving a weather report, a recipe requiring a temperature adjustment, a scientific experiment, and a storage condition.

Evidence-to-action: For learners who calculate correctly but misinterpret the result, add a final sentence prompt: “This temperature means ______ in the given context.”

SESSION 4 — Integrated Temperature Conversion and Mastery

Daily objective: Select, perform, justify, and communicate appropriate temperature conversions in multi-step real-world situations.

ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Formula sort: match Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin situations with the correct conversion relationship.

ANALYSIS

Reasoning

Analyze two competing solutions and defend which one is correct using formulas, units, and contextual reasonableness.

ABSTRACTION

Synthesis

Reinforce that temperature conversion is a function: an input temperature is transformed into an equivalent value on another scale.

APPLICATION

Mastery

Complete an integrated scenario involving weather, food preparation, scientific measurement, and storage. Explain each conversion.

Evidence-to-action: Use the mastery evidence to identify the failed step and give a parallel problem rather than repeating an entire worksheet.

LEARNER GUIDE

Temperature Conversions

Understand the scales, formulas, and practical applications.

What You Will Learn

  • How Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin are related.
  • How to convert Celsius to Fahrenheit and back.
  • How to convert Celsius to Kelvin and back.
  • How temperature conversions are used in recipes, weather, science, storage, and energy contexts.
  • How to check whether an answer is reasonable.

Essential Definitions

TermMeaning
Celsius (°C)A temperature scale commonly used in everyday life and science.
Fahrenheit (°F)A temperature scale commonly used in some countries for weather and household measurements.
Kelvin (K)An absolute temperature scale used extensively in science; Kelvin values do not use a degree symbol.
Temperature conversionChanging a temperature value from one scale to an equivalent value on another scale.

Key Formulas

°F = (9/5)(°C) + 32
°C = (5/9)(°F − 32)
K = °C + 273.15
°C = K − 273.15

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

1. Celsius to Fahrenheit

Convert 25°C to Fahrenheit.

°F = (9/5)(25) + 32 = 45 + 32 = 77°F.

2. Fahrenheit to Celsius

Convert 86°F to Celsius.

°C = (5/9)(86 − 32) = (5/9)(54) = 30°C.

3. Celsius to Kelvin

Convert 25°C to Kelvin.

K = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K.

4. Kelvin to Celsius

Convert 300 K to Celsius.

°C = 300 − 273.15 = 26.85°C.

5. Real-World Application

A weather report gives 95°F. Convert it to Celsius.

°C = (5/9)(95 − 32) = (5/9)(63) = 35°C. This is a hot outdoor temperature.

Common Mistakes & Pitfalls

  • Using the Celsius-to-Fahrenheit formula when converting Fahrenheit to Celsius.
  • Forgetting to subtract 32 before multiplying by 5/9.
  • Adding 273.15 when converting Kelvin to Celsius instead of subtracting it.
  • Writing °K instead of K.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Giving a mathematically correct value without interpreting what it means in context.

Real-World Connections

Temperature conversions are used when following recipes written for a different temperature scale, interpreting weather reports, conducting scientific experiments, checking storage conditions, and comparing temperature information used in energy-related systems.

Quick Self-Check

  1. Convert 20°C to Fahrenheit.
  2. Convert 68°F to Celsius.
  3. Convert 27°C to Kelvin.
  4. Convert 310 K to Celsius.
  5. Why must you identify the starting and target temperature scales before choosing a formula?

TEACHER NOTES

Support, Remediation and Enrichment

Common Learner Misconceptions

  • Assuming identical numerical values represent identical temperatures across scales.
  • Using the wrong direction of conversion.
  • Forgetting the +32 or −32 operation.
  • Confusing Kelvin notation with Celsius/Fahrenheit notation.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Ignoring the real-world meaning of the final answer.

Remediation

  • Use a three-column scale chart: source, formula, target.
  • Have learners identify source and target before touching a calculator.
  • Color-code the operation sequence on the formula card.
  • Use benchmark temperatures such as 0°C, 20°C, 25°C, and 100°C for reasonableness checks.
  • Give one-step parallel problems after each observed error.

Enrichment

  • Compare daily temperatures reported in Celsius and Fahrenheit.
  • Create a cooking conversion chart for common oven temperatures.
  • Investigate why Kelvin is useful in science.
  • Design a simple spreadsheet temperature converter.
