BOW Learning Competency:
Solve problems involving salaries, wages, benefits, and deductions (tax computations, overtime pay, and gross and net incomes) using appropriate technology.

Intentions

Overall learning intention: Learners will interpret payroll information, calculate earnings and deductions, determine gross and net income, and use appropriate technology to check and communicate results.

Observable objectives for the week

  • Identify and distinguish salary, wage, overtime pay, benefits, deductions, gross income, and net income.
  • Calculate regular earnings, overtime earnings, benefits, gross income, specified deductions, and net income.
  • Apply a stated instructional tax schedule correctly and verify computations using a calculator or spreadsheet.
  • Analyze payroll errors and explain whether a calculated net income is reasonable.
Tax examples in this package use a clearly stated instructional tax schedule created for classroom computation practice. It is not a current legal tax table.

SESSION 1 — Salary, Wage and Gross Earnings

Daily objective: By the end of the session, learners will classify salary and wage information and calculate regular earnings and gross earnings from given payroll data.

A1 • ACTIVITY

Retrieval + Context

Display four short payroll situations: monthly salary, hourly wage, daily wage, and piece-based earning. Learners identify the time or work basis and estimate which worker earns more for the stated period.

Evidence: 4-item quick sort and one estimation.

A2 • ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Pairs compare answers and explain why the time basis matters. Teacher probes: “What information is fixed? What information changes?” Address common confusion between monthly salary and monthly gross income.

Evidence: Oral justification + corrected sort.

A3 • ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Teacher models: annual → monthly; monthly → weekly using the stated assumptions; hourly rate × regular hours; gross earnings = regular earnings + specified additional earnings/benefits.

Gross income = Regular earnings + Overtime pay + Applicable benefits/allowances

A4 • APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Learners solve a payroll card with a monthly salary, one allowance and regular work hours. They verify using a calculator or spreadsheet and write one sentence interpreting the result.

Exit evidence: one independent payroll computation.

Ways Forward: Learners below the agreed accuracy threshold receive a partially completed payroll table next session; learners who demonstrate mastery receive a reverse problem where they determine a missing salary component.

SESSION 2 — Overtime Pay and Benefits

Daily objective: By the end of the session, learners will calculate overtime pay and specified benefits and integrate them into gross income.

A1 • ACTIVITY

Worked-example comparison

Learners compare two overtime computations, one correct and one using the regular hourly rate for overtime hours. They decide which is reasonable and explain why.

Evidence: Error-selection response.

A2 • ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Teacher guides learners to identify the base hourly rate, overtime multiplier, overtime hours, and overtime pay. Learners annotate each quantity in a sample payroll.

Evidence: Annotated computation.

A3 • ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
Overtime rate = Regular hourly rate × stated overtime multiplier
Overtime pay = Overtime rate × Overtime hours

Benefits/allowances are added to gross income only when the problem states that they form part of the gross earnings.

A4 • APPLICATION

Collaborative → Independent

Teams complete a payroll scenario containing regular hours, overtime hours and a stated allowance. Each learner then completes a parallel problem independently and checks the result with technology.

Ways Forward: If learners confuse rate and total pay, reteach using a “rate × quantity = amount” frame. If accurate, extend to a reverse problem finding missing overtime hours.

SESSION 3 — Deductions, Instructional Tax and Net Income

Daily objective: By the end of the session, learners will calculate stated deductions and use the instructional tax schedule to determine net income.

A1 • ACTIVITY

Prior Knowledge

Give a sample payslip with gross income and several deductions. Learners predict whether net income should be greater or less than gross income and identify which values must be subtracted.

Evidence: Prediction + deduction sort.

A2 • ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Pairs compare two net-income calculations. One subtracts a deduction twice. Learners identify the error and explain the effect on net income.

Evidence: Written error explanation.

A3 • ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Teacher models a stated instructional tax schedule and emphasizes the sequence: determine taxable base according to the problem → compute tax → total deductions → subtract deductions from gross income.

Net income = Gross income − Total deductions

A4 • APPLICATION

Technology-supported practice

Learners compute net income from a payslip using a calculator or spreadsheet. They compare their result with a peer and explain one reason the result is reasonable.

Ways Forward: Provide a deduction checklist for learners who omit deductions. Learners who demonstrate mastery compare two payslips and determine which worker has the higher net income and why.

SESSION 4 — Integrated Payroll Problem and Mastery

Daily objective: By the end of the session, learners will solve and explain an integrated payroll problem involving regular earnings, overtime, benefits, deductions, instructional tax, gross income and net income.

A1 • ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Four quick prompts: identify the gross-income components, identify deductions, select the overtime formula, and state the relationship between gross and net income.

Evidence: 4-item retrieval check.

A2 • ANALYSIS

Metacognitive Check

Learners inspect a deliberately flawed payroll and mark each step as correct/incorrect. They justify the correction rather than simply giving a new answer.

Evidence: Error-analysis table.

A3 • ABSTRACTION

Synthesis

Class builds a payroll flow:

Regular earnings → Overtime/benefits → Gross income → Tax/deductions → Net income

Teacher emphasizes that the order and stated assumptions must be followed.

A4 • APPLICATION

Mastery Task

Individual integrated payroll case. Learners compute, use technology to verify, and write a short interpretation of the net income.

Mastery evidence: accurate computation + explanation + reasonableness check.

Ways Forward: Classify evidence as Not Yet Mastered, Developing, or Mastered. Provide targeted remediation on the specific failed step; provide a transfer task for learners who demonstrate mastery.

LEARNER GUIDE

Salaries, Benefits and Deductions

Four-session discussion and study guide.

What You Will Learn

  • Distinguish salary, wage, overtime pay, benefits, deductions, gross income and net income.
  • Calculate regular and overtime earnings.
  • Combine specified earnings and benefits to obtain gross income.
  • Calculate stated deductions and instructional tax.
  • Determine and interpret net income.
  • Use a calculator or spreadsheet to verify computations.

Essential Definitions

TermMeaning
SalaryA stated amount of pay for a specified period, commonly monthly or annually.
WagePay commonly based on time worked or units completed.
Overtime payAdditional pay for hours worked beyond the stated regular hours, using the multiplier given in the problem.
Benefit/AllowanceAn additional employment-related amount included only when the problem states how it contributes to earnings.
DeductionAn amount subtracted from applicable earnings.
Gross incomeTotal applicable earnings before the stated deductions.
Net incomeAmount remaining after the applicable deductions are subtracted.

Key Ideas and Formulas

Regular earnings = Regular rate × Regular hours
Overtime rate = Regular hourly rate × Overtime multiplier
Overtime pay = Overtime rate × Overtime hours
Gross income = Regular earnings + Overtime pay + Applicable benefits/allowances
Total deductions = Tax + Other stated deductions
Net income = Gross income − Total deductions
Always use the assumptions given in the problem. Do not substitute a real-world payroll rule unless it is explicitly provided.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Regular Earnings

A worker earns ₱120 per hour and works 40 regular hours.

Regular earnings = ₱120 × 40 = ₱4,800.

Example 2 — Overtime

The regular hourly rate is ₱150. Overtime is paid at 125% of the regular rate for 4 hours.

Overtime rate = ₱150 × 1.25 = ₱187.50
Overtime pay = ₱187.50 × 4 = ₱750.

Example 3 — Gross Income

Regular earnings are ₱12,000, overtime pay is ₱1,000, and an applicable allowance is ₱500.

Gross income = ₱12,000 + ₱1,000 + ₱500 = ₱13,500.

Example 4 — Net Income

Gross income is ₱18,000. Under the instructional tax schedule, tax is 5% of gross income. Other stated deductions total ₱600.

Tax = ₱18,000 × 0.05 = ₱900
Total deductions = ₱900 + ₱600 = ₱1,500
Net income = ₱18,000 − ₱1,500 = ₱16,500.

Common Mistakes and Pitfalls

  1. Using regular pay for overtime hours instead of the stated overtime multiplier.
  2. Adding a deduction to gross income instead of subtracting it.
  3. Calling gross income the same as net income.
  4. Applying tax to the wrong amount when the problem provides a specific base.
  5. Rounding too early.
  6. Trusting a calculator output without checking whether the operation and units are sensible.

Real-World Connection

Understanding payroll helps a person interpret a payslip, plan spending, compare employment offers, check computations and identify questions that should be clarified with an employer or payroll office.

Quick Self-Check

  1. What is the difference between gross and net income?
  2. Why is an overtime multiplier applied to the regular hourly rate?
  3. Which direction should deductions move net income?
  4. What should you do when a calculator result seems unreasonable?

TEACHER NOTES

Instructional Support and Ways Forward

Use evidence from the four sessions to adjust the next learning experience.

Common Learner Misconceptions

  • Confusing salary with gross income.
  • Using the regular hourly rate for overtime without the stated multiplier.
  • Adding deductions instead of subtracting them.
  • Applying tax before determining the required income base.
  • Assuming every benefit is automatically taxable or deductible without checking the problem's stated assumptions.
  • Accepting calculator output without checking whether the answer is reasonable.

Remediation

  1. Use a payroll vocabulary matching activity.
  2. Provide a color-coded payroll flow: earnings → gross → deductions → net.
  3. Use “rate × quantity = amount” for overtime.
  4. Start with one deduction before adding multiple deductions.
  5. Use partially completed examples before independent problems.
  6. Reassess the exact failed step rather than repeating the entire worksheet.

Enrichment

  1. Ask learners to design a simplified payslip.
  2. Compare two workers with different gross income and deductions.
  3. Reverse a payroll problem to find a missing value.
  4. Create a spreadsheet formula that automates net-income calculation.
  5. Ask learners to explain why a higher gross income does not always guarantee a proportionally higher net income.
