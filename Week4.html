SESSION 1 — Sequence vs Series

Daily objective: Distinguish a sequence from a series and explain the difference using mathematical examples.

ACTIVITY

Activate

Display: 2, 5, 8, 11, … and 2 + 5 + 8 + 11. Learners identify what is being shown and what changes when plus signs are inserted.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Pairs sort examples into “sequence” or “series” and justify the classification. Address the misconception that a series is simply another name for a sequence.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

A sequence is an ordered list of terms. A series is the indicated sum of the terms of a sequence.

Sequence: 3, 6, 9, 12, …
Series: 3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + …

APPLICATION

Independent

Learners classify six representations and convert three sequences into corresponding series. Exit ticket: explain the difference in one sentence.

Ways forward: If learners confuse the concepts, return to “list” versus “sum” representations. If secure, introduce finite and infinite notation conceptually.

SESSION 2 — Arithmetic and Geometric Series + Sigma Notation

Daily objective: Illustrate arithmetic and geometric series and represent finite series using sigma notation and vice versa.

ACTIVITY

Connect

Recall arithmetic/geometric sequences. Convert 4, 7, 10, 13 into a sum and 3, 6, 12, 24 into a sum. Learners identify the generating pattern.

ANALYSIS

Compare

Determine whether the terms in each sum have a constant difference or ratio. Examine how the index in sigma notation tells how many terms are included.

ABSTRACTION

Model

Introduce sigma notation as a compact way to write a sum.

Σ aₖ, from k = 1 to n means a₁ + a₂ + … + aₙ
Arithmetic series: Σ[a₁ + (k − 1)d]
Geometric series: Σ[a₁rᵏ⁻¹]

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Write four finite series in sigma notation and expand four sigma expressions. Learners identify whether each is arithmetic or geometric.

Ways forward: Scaffold with a term-index table. Enrichment: write two different-looking sigma expressions for the same finite sum and explain the equivalence.

SESSION 3 — Finding Sums of Arithmetic and Geometric Series

Daily objective: Calculate finite arithmetic and geometric series sums using the appropriate formula and verify the result.

ACTIVITY

Retrieve

Quick classification: identify a₁, d, r and n from four examples. Learners predict whether the sum formula should use d or r.

ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Compare a correct and incorrect solution. Learners locate whether the error came from classification, substitution, exponent, or arithmetic.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
Arithmetic: Sₙ = n/2 [2a₁ + (n − 1)d]
Geometric (r ≠ 1): Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)

Model substitution and a reasonableness check.

APPLICATION

Practice

Solve three arithmetic-series and three geometric-series sums. Learners label known values before substituting.

Ways forward: For learners who select the wrong formula, reteach classification before computation. For accurate learners, add missing-value problems.

SESSION 4 — Contextual Problems Involving Series

Daily objective: Select, apply and interpret an arithmetic or geometric series model in a real-world problem.

ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Four-minute formula sprint: identify whether each situation involves a constant addition or constant multiplication and select the matching series model.

ANALYSIS

Model Selection

Discuss evidence in two contexts: a fixed increase in weekly savings and repeated multiplication in production/growth. Learners explain why one model fits each.

ABSTRACTION

Synthesis
Read context → list terms → identify d or r → determine n → choose series formula → substitute → calculate → interpret/check

APPLICATION

Mastery

Independently solve one arithmetic-series and one geometric-series contextual problem. Show model, formula, substitution, answer and interpretation.

Ways forward: Remediate the exact failed step. Learners demonstrating mastery create a context that can be modeled by a finite series and justify the model.

LEARNER GUIDE

Arithmetic and Geometric Series

Four-session study guide.

What You Will Learn

  • Differentiate sequences and series.
  • Recognize arithmetic and geometric series.
  • Use sigma notation.
  • Find finite sums.
  • Apply series to practical problems.

Essential Definitions

ConceptMeaning
SequenceAn ordered list of terms.
SeriesThe indicated sum of the terms of a sequence.
Arithmetic seriesA series formed from an arithmetic sequence with common difference d.
Geometric seriesA series formed from a geometric sequence with common ratio r.
Sigma notationA compact notation used to represent a sum.

Key Formulas

Arithmetic series: Sₙ = n/2 [2a₁ + (n − 1)d]
Equivalent arithmetic form: Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2
Geometric series, r ≠ 1: Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)
Sigma: Σ aₖ, k = 1 to n = a₁ + a₂ + … + aₙ

Worked Examples

1. Arithmetic Series

Find the sum of 4 + 7 + 10 + … + 31.

a₁ = 4, aₙ = 31, d = 3. Find n: 31 = 4 + (n − 1)3 → 27 = 3(n − 1) → n = 10. Then S₁₀ = 10(4 + 31)/2 = 175.

2. Geometric Series

Find 3 + 6 + 12 + 24 + 48.

a₁ = 3, r = 2, n = 5. S₅ = 3(1 − 2⁵)/(1 − 2) = 3(−31)/(−1) = 93.

3. Sigma Notation

Write 5 + 8 + 11 + 14 + 17 in sigma notation.

a₁ = 5, d = 3. General term = 5 + 3(k − 1). Therefore: Σ[5 + 3(k − 1)] from k = 1 to 5.

4. Context

A learner saves ₱200 in week 1 and increases the weekly saving by ₱50 for 8 weeks. Find the total saved.

S₈ = 8/2[2(₱200) + 7(₱50)] = 4[₱400 + ₱350] = ₱3,000.

Common Mistakes

  • Calling an ordered list a series without indicating a sum.
  • Using the arithmetic-series formula for a geometric series.
  • Using n when n − 1 is required in the term formula.
  • Forgetting the exponent n in the geometric sum.
  • Writing an incorrect sigma general term.
  • Failing to interpret a contextual answer.

Real-World Connections

Finite series can represent accumulated savings, repeated production amounts, staged arrangements, and other situations where a quantity follows a regular additive or multiplicative pattern.

Quick Self-Check

  1. What is the difference between a sequence and a series?
  2. How do you recognize an arithmetic series?
  3. How do you recognize a geometric series?
  4. What does the sigma index represent?
  5. Which formula is used for a finite arithmetic series?
  6. Which formula is used for a finite geometric series when r ≠ 1?

  2. 2 + 5 + 8 + 11.
  3. Arithmetic; common difference d = 3.
  4. d = 9 − 5 = 4.
  5. r = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
  6. 2(1)+1 + 2(2)+1 + 2(3)+1 + 2(4)+1 = 3 + 5 + 7 + 9.

  8. a₁ = 2, r = 3, n = 5. S₅ = 2(1 − 3⁵)/(1 − 3) = 2(−242)/(−2) = 242.
  9. Σ[6 + 4(k − 1)], k = 1 to 5.
  10. a₁ = 10, d = 5, n = 8. S₈ = 8/2[20 + 35] = 220.
  11. a₁ = 5, r = 2, n = 6. S₆ = 5(1 − 2⁶)/(1 − 2) = 315.
  12. S₆ = 6/2[2(₱250) + 5(₱50)] = 3[₱500 + ₱250] = ₱2,250.
  13. Error: the differences are not constant. The terms double, so it is geometric with r = 2.
  14. 50 = 5 + (n − 1)3 → 45 = 3(n − 1) → n − 1 = 15 → n = 16.

  17. S₁₂ = 12/2[2(₱100) + 11(₱25)] = 6[₱200 + ₱275] = ₱2,850.

TEACHER NOTES

Support, Remediation and Enrichment

Common Misconceptions

  • Sequence and series are interchangeable terms.
  • Arithmetic means “adding any amount,” rather than a constant difference.
  • Geometric means “increasing,” rather than a constant ratio.
  • Incorrect sigma index or general term.
  • Confusing nth term with sum of n terms.
  • Using the wrong sum formula.

Remediation

  • Use term cards and physically arrange them as a list, then add them as a sum.
  • Place differences or ratios underneath consecutive terms.
  • Use a formula-selection decision tree.
  • Build sigma notation from a term-index table.
  • Reassess the failed step, not just the whole worksheet.

Enrichment

  • Derive or explain the arithmetic-series formula using pairing.
  • Compare two different representations of the same sum.
  • Use a spreadsheet to generate a finite series and verify its sum.
  • Create a real-world problem that requires choosing between arithmetic and geometric series.
