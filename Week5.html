SESSION 1 — Financial Patterns: Payments and Growth

Daily objective: Identify the sequence or series structure in recurring payments and investment-growth situations and explain the role of the terms.

ACTIVITY

Elicit

Present two situations: (A) a person pays ₱2,000 every month for 6 months; (B) an investment grows by 5% each period. Learners list the amounts and identify what stays constant or changes.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Compare constant-payment and repeated-growth patterns. Ask: Is the financial quantity represented by equal additions, equal multiplications, or a sum of periodic amounts?

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling

Repeated equal payments form an arithmetic/constant sequence of payments, while repeated percentage growth forms a geometric sequence. A total paid or accumulated amount is represented by a series.

Total of equal payments = payment × number of payments
Growth: Aₙ = P(1 + r)ⁿ

APPLICATION

Independent

Learners model three short financial situations, identify sequence/series type, and state whether they need a term or a sum. Exit ticket: distinguish “amount at one period” from “total over several periods.”

Evidence-to-action: If learners confuse a term with a total, use a timeline showing one balance versus the sum of several payments or deposits.

SESSION 2 — Periodic Payments for Loans and Mortgages

Daily objective: Represent a simplified periodic-payment situation using a sequence/series and calculate the total of the stated payments.

ACTIVITY

Connect

Analyze a six-payment example: ₱3,000 paid every month. Learners build a payment table and calculate the total amount paid before discussing interest.

ANALYSIS

Error Analysis

Compare “monthly payment” with “total payments.” Learners identify why multiplying the payment by the number of periods gives the total only when the payment is constant and the stated problem asks for the sum of payments.

ABSTRACTION

Scaffold

For a simplified equal-payment model, the payment sequence is constant. The series is the sum of all scheduled payments.

Sₙ = a + a + … + a = na

For finance problems with changing payment amounts, use the appropriate arithmetic/geometric-series structure given by the situation.

APPLICATION

Guided → Independent

Solve equal-payment loan examples and one changing-payment example. Learners show the payment sequence, identify n, then calculate the total scheduled payments.

Teacher response: Separate the classroom model from a real lender's amortization schedule. Learners must not assume that “total payments” equals principal unless the problem explicitly defines it that way.

SESSION 3 — Accumulated Value of Investments

Daily objective: Determine the accumulated value of an investment after repeated growth and use a geometric series when several deposits or accumulated amounts are involved.

ACTIVITY

Retrieve

Starting with ₱10,000 at 5% annual growth, calculate the value after one, two, and three periods. Learners identify the common ratio.

ANALYSIS

Formative Check

Discuss why 5% growth means multiplying by 1.05 rather than adding ₱5. Compare a single investment with repeated deposits.

ABSTRACTION

Explicit Modeling
Single amount after n periods: A = P(1 + r)ⁿ
Geometric sum: Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), r ≠ 1

When each period contributes an amount that then grows, the resulting accumulated contributions can form a geometric series.

APPLICATION

Practice

Calculate values for a single investment and for repeated equal deposits in simplified scenarios. Learners explain which quantity is a term and which is a sum.

Ways forward: If the exponent is misplaced, use a period table. If learners are secure, introduce the distinction between nominal contribution and accumulated contribution.

SESSION 4 — Integrated Financial Problem Solving

Daily objective: Select and apply an appropriate sequence/series model to solve and interpret an integrated loan or investment problem.

ACTIVITY

Retrieval

Formula-selection sprint: identify whether each situation requires a constant-payment total, arithmetic sum, geometric growth, or geometric sum.

ANALYSIS

Reasoning

Learners annotate a word problem: known amount, rate, period, payment/deposit, requested value. Discuss why the model matches the wording.

ABSTRACTION

Synthesis
Read → identify financial quantities → write first terms → identify pattern → choose formula → substitute → compute → interpret → check reasonableness.

APPLICATION

Mastery

Independently solve one periodic-payment problem and one investment-growth problem. Require complete solution, units/currency, and a one-sentence interpretation.

Evidence-to-action: Classify learners as Not Yet Mastered, Developing, or Mastered. Reteach the exact failed modeling or computation step before reassessment.

LEARNER GUIDE

Financial Applications of Sequences and Series

Study guide for loans, periodic payments, and investments.

What You Will Learn

  • How recurring payments can be represented as sequences and series.
  • How to calculate the total of equal periodic payments.
  • How repeated percentage growth forms a geometric sequence.
  • How a geometric series can represent accumulated repeated contributions.
  • How to choose a mathematical model and interpret a financial result.

Essential Definitions

TermMeaning
Periodic paymentAn amount paid at regular intervals.
InvestmentMoney placed into an asset or account with the expectation of future value.
Accumulated valueThe value of an amount after growth over one or more periods.
Growth factorThe multiplier used after a percentage increase. For rate r, the factor is 1 + r.
Geometric sequenceA sequence in which consecutive terms have a constant ratio.
Geometric seriesThe indicated sum of the terms of a geometric sequence.

Key Formulas

Equal periodic payments: Total = payment × number of payments
Compound growth for one initial amount: A = P(1 + r)ⁿ
Finite geometric series: Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), r ≠ 1
Finite arithmetic series: Sₙ = n/2[2a₁ + (n − 1)d]

Important: Use the formula that matches the structure described in the problem. A real loan's amortization schedule may require an annuity model and may not be represented by simple multiplication of a payment by n when interest is involved.

Worked Examples

1. Equal Periodic Payments

A simplified payment plan requires ₱2,500 every month for 8 months. Find the total scheduled payments.

There are 8 equal payments: S = 8(₱2,500) = ₱20,000.

2. Investment Growth

An investment of ₱10,000 grows at 5% per year. Find its value after 3 years.

A = ₱10,000(1.05)³ = ₱10,000(1.157625) = ₱11,576.25.

3. Repeated Contributions

Suppose ₱1,000 is invested at the end of each period and, for a classroom model, each earlier contribution is multiplied by 1.05 per period. After 4 periods, the accumulated contributions are modeled by 1,000(1.05)³ + 1,000(1.05)² + 1,000(1.05) + 1,000.

This is a geometric series with first term 1,000, ratio 1.05, and 4 terms. S₄ = 1,000[(1.05⁴ − 1)/(1.05 − 1)] = ₱4,310.125.

4. Choosing the Model

If a situation says “the amount increases by 5% each year,” use a multiplicative growth model. If it says “₱2,000 is paid every month,” the payment total is a constant-payment sum.

Common Mistakes

  • Adding 5% as 0.05 instead of multiplying by 1.05 for growth.
  • Confusing one accumulated value with the total of several accumulated contributions.
  • Using a geometric formula for equal payments without checking the problem structure.
  • Ignoring the number of periods.
  • Reporting a numerical result without ₱ or another appropriate unit.
  • Assuming a simplified classroom payment model is identical to a real amortization schedule.

Real-World Connections

Sequences and series help describe recurring payments, savings plans, investment growth, loan schedules, and other repeated financial processes. The key mathematical skill is matching the structure of the financial situation to the correct model.

Quick Self-Check

  1. What is the difference between a payment and the total of payments?
  2. What is the growth factor for a 6% increase?
  3. What formula models one initial amount growing for n periods?
  4. When would a geometric series be useful for accumulated contributions?
  5. Why should you inspect the wording before choosing a formula?

TEACHER NOTES

Support, Remediation and Enrichment

Common Misconceptions

  • Thinking a percentage rate is the same as the growth factor.
  • Confusing a single future value with the sum of several contributions.
  • Assuming all loans can be solved by payment × number of periods.
  • Using a geometric-series formula when payments are simply equal.
  • Forgetting that the exponent counts growth periods.

Remediation

  • Use a timeline for each period.
  • Write the first three terms before choosing a formula.
  • Color-code P, r, n, and A in worked examples.
  • Use a decision table: equal payments → constant sum; repeated percentage growth → geometric model.
  • Have learners explain the meaning of their answer before moving to another problem.

Enrichment

  • Compare equal periodic payments with growing payments.
  • Use a spreadsheet to generate investment-growth values.
  • Investigate how changing the growth rate changes accumulated value.
  • Extend the classroom model to annuity concepts after the core BOW competency is secure.
