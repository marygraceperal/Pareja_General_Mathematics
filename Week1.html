SESSION 1

Salary and Wage Foundations

From employment information to weekly and monthly earnings.

FORMATIVE EVIDENCE

Daily Objectives

  • Define and distinguish salary and wage in employment contexts.
  • Calculate monthly and weekly earnings from an annual salary using stated assumptions.
  • Interpret the meaning of a calculated wage in a simple payroll situation.
1. ACTIVITY [Prior Knowledge] [Diagnostic Evidence]

Payroll Information Sort

Present learners with short employment cards showing examples such as: "₱360,000 per year," "₱650 per day," "₱80 per hour," and "₱25,000 monthly."

Learner task: In pairs, classify the information as salary-based, hourly wage, daily wage, or monthly salary. Learners explain what additional information would be needed to determine earnings for a particular pay period.

Teacher evidence: Listen for whether learners recognize the difference between a stated annual salary and a rate of pay.

Retrieval Practice Diagnostic Questioning Collaborative Learning

2. ANALYSIS [Questioning] [Misconception Check]
  1. What information tells us that an amount is an annual salary?
  2. Why can we not call ₱80 per hour an annual salary?
  3. If two workers have the same annual salary, will they always receive the same amount every pay period?
  4. What assumption must we make when converting an annual salary to a monthly amount?
  5. What assumption must we make when estimating a weekly amount from an annual salary?
Likely misconception: Learners may treat every pay amount as interchangeable without identifying the time period represented by the number.
3. ABSTRACTION [Explicit Modeling] [Worked Example]

Salary is commonly stated as a fixed amount for a longer period, such as a year. A wage is commonly expressed according to a time or work basis such as an hour or day.

Monthly salary = Annual salary ÷ 12
Weekly equivalent = Annual salary ÷ 52
Worked Example

Given: Annual salary = ₱360,000

Monthly salary:

₱360,000 ÷ 12 = ₱30,000

Weekly equivalent:

₱360,000 ÷ 52 ≈ ₱6,923.08

Interpretation: Under these assumptions, the annual salary corresponds to ₱30,000 per month and approximately ₱6,923.08 per week.

Explicit Modeling Worked Example
4. APPLICATION [Guided Practice] [Transfer]

A worker earns an annual salary of ₱468,000.

  1. Determine the monthly salary.
  2. Determine the weekly equivalent using 52 weeks.
  3. Explain why the weekly figure is an equivalent rather than necessarily the exact amount appearing on every payslip.

Expected evidence: Correct setup, computation, rounding, and explanation.

Evidence-to-Action

Evidence What It Shows Teacher Response Next-Session Implication
Classification and conversion responses Whether learners understand pay periods and basic conversion. Clarify time-period language or model another conversion if needed. Begin Session 2 with a short retrieval check before hourly and overtime calculations.

Exit Evidence

Exit ticket: "A worker earns ₱312,000 annually. What is the monthly salary? What operation did you use and why?"

SESSION 2

Hourly Wages, Overtime, and Allowances

Using rates and additional work information to determine earnings.

FORMATIVE EVIDENCE

Daily Objectives

  • Calculate regular earnings from an hourly rate and number of hours worked.
  • Calculate overtime earnings using a stated overtime rate.
  • Determine total earnings when regular pay, overtime pay, and allowances are combined.
1. ACTIVITY [Retrieval] [Contextualized Problem]

Who Earned More?

Worker A earns ₱80 per hour and works 40 regular hours. Worker B earns ₱75 per hour, works 40 regular hours, and receives a ₱500 allowance.

Learners calculate the earnings of each worker and discuss what information is necessary before saying which worker earned more.

Retrieval Practice Comparison
2. ANALYSIS [Reasoning] [Error Analysis]
  1. How did you calculate regular earnings?
  2. Why is an allowance added to earnings?
  3. What makes overtime different from regular work?
  4. If the overtime rate is 1.25 times the regular hourly rate, what does that mean?
  5. A learner says: "Overtime pay means adding 25 pesos to the hourly rate." Is this always correct? Explain.
3. ABSTRACTION [Explicit Modeling] [Worked Example]
Regular earnings = Regular hourly rate × Regular hours
Overtime rate = Regular hourly rate × Overtime multiplier
Overtime earnings = Overtime rate × Overtime hours
Total earnings = Regular earnings + Overtime earnings + Allowances
Worked Example

Regular rate = ₱100/hour

Regular hours = 40

Overtime hours = 5

Overtime multiplier = 1.25

Allowance = ₱500

Regular earnings:

₱100 × 40 = ₱4,000

Overtime rate:

₱100 × 1.25 = ₱125/hour

Overtime earnings:

₱125 × 5 = ₱625

Total earnings:

₱4,000 + ₱625 + ₱500 = ₱5,125

Important: Overtime rules and rates depend on the applicable employment rules and the problem's stated assumptions. Use the rate supplied in the learning task rather than assuming one universal rate.
4. APPLICATION [Problem Solving] [Error Analysis]

Maria earns ₱95 per hour. She works 40 regular hours and 4 overtime hours. Her overtime multiplier is 1.25. She also receives a ₱300 allowance.

  1. Calculate her regular earnings.
  2. Calculate her overtime rate.
  3. Calculate her overtime earnings.
  4. Calculate her total earnings.
  5. Explain why the allowance is included in total earnings in this example.

Support: Provide a partially completed computation table to learners who need scaffolding.

Extension: Ask learners to compare the total earnings with a worker who receives no overtime but has a higher hourly rate.

Evidence-to-Action

Evidence What It Shows Teacher Response Next-Session Implication
Overtime computation and explanation Whether learners can distinguish rate, hours, multiplier, and total earnings. Model the rate calculation again if learners add the multiplier incorrectly. Session 3 begins with a short comparison between time-based earnings and performance-based earnings.

Exit Evidence

A worker earns ₱90/hour and works 3 overtime hours at 1.25 times the regular rate. Determine the overtime earnings.

SESSION 3

Commission and Piecework Earnings

Understanding earnings based on sales or units completed.

FORMATIVE EVIDENCE

Daily Objectives

  • Calculate commission earnings from a stated sales amount and commission rate.
  • Calculate piecework earnings from a stated rate per unit and quantity completed.
  • Compare different earning arrangements and justify a calculation.
1. ACTIVITY [Comparison] [Collaborative Learning]

Present three earning arrangements:

  • Worker A: ₱600 per day
  • Worker B: 5% commission on ₱12,000 sales
  • Worker C: ₱15 per completed item and 500 items completed

Learners estimate and calculate the earnings under each arrangement.

2. ANALYSIS [Reasoning] [Concept Formation]
  1. What quantity determines commission earnings?
  2. What quantity determines piecework earnings?
  3. Why is the commission rate written as a percentage?
  4. Why is a piecework rate multiplied by the number of completed units?
  5. Which arrangement produces the greatest earning in the examples? What evidence supports your conclusion?
3. ABSTRACTION [Explicit Modeling] [Worked Example]
Commission = Sales × Commission rate
Piecework earnings = Rate per unit × Number of units completed
Commission Example

Sales = ₱24,000

Commission rate = 5%

5% = 0.05

Commission = ₱24,000 × 0.05

Commission = ₱1,200

Piecework Example

Rate = ₱18 per completed item

Completed items = 350

Piecework earnings = ₱18 × 350

Piecework earnings = ₱6,300

4. APPLICATION [Transfer] [Decision Making]

A salesperson receives 4% commission on sales. During one period, the salesperson makes ₱35,000 in sales.

Another worker earns ₱20 for every completed item and completes 1,500 items.

  1. Calculate the salesperson's commission.
  2. Calculate the piecework worker's earnings.
  3. Compare the two amounts.
  4. Explain why the calculation method is different for the two workers.

Extension: Ask learners to determine the sales amount needed for a 4% commission to equal ₱2,000.

Evidence-to-Action

Evidence What It Shows Teacher Response Next-Session Implication
Commission and piecework solutions Whether learners select the correct base quantity and operation. Reteach the meaning of "rate" and "base" if learners multiply by the wrong quantity. Session 4 integrates earnings with benefits and deductions to construct a fuller payroll picture.

Exit Evidence

A worker receives 6% commission on ₱18,000 sales. Calculate the commission and explain the operation used.

SESSION 4

Benefits, Deductions, Gross Income, and Net Income

Putting the week's learning together in a payroll case.

MASTERY EVIDENCE

Daily Objectives

  • Distinguish earnings, benefits, and deductions in a simplified payroll scenario.
  • Calculate gross income and net income using the amounts provided in a payroll case.
  • Evaluate a payroll computation and explain whether the final amount is reasonable.
1. ACTIVITY [Integration] [Diagnostic Review]

Build the Payslip

Provide the following simplified payroll information:

  • Basic earnings: ₱18,000
  • Overtime earnings: ₱1,500
  • Allowance: ₱1,000
  • Benefit included in the problem: ₱500
  • Deduction A: ₱900
  • Deduction B: ₱600

Learners sort the amounts into earnings, benefits, and deductions before calculating the final amounts.

2. ANALYSIS [Classification] [Reasoning]
  1. Which amounts increase the worker's total earnings?
  2. Which amounts reduce the amount received after deductions?
  3. Why should we identify the role of each amount before adding or subtracting?
  4. What is the difference between gross income and net income?
  5. Why might the amount received by a worker be lower than gross income?
Important: Actual payroll treatment of benefits and deductions depends on the applicable employment and tax rules. In classroom problems, use the classification and assumptions explicitly provided by the task.
3. ABSTRACTION [Explicit Modeling] [Worked Example]
Gross income = Earnings + applicable benefits/allowances included by the problem
Total deductions = Deduction 1 + Deduction 2 + ...
Net income = Gross income − Total deductions
Worked Payroll Example

Basic earnings = ₱18,000

Overtime = ₱1,500

Allowance = ₱1,000

Included benefit = ₱500

Deductions = ₱900 + ₱600 = ₱1,500

Gross income:

₱18,000 + ₱1,500 + ₱1,000 + ₱500 = ₱21,000

Net income:

₱21,000 − ₱1,500 = ₱19,500

Interpretation: Under the simplified assumptions of the problem, the worker's gross income is ₱21,000 and the amount after the stated deductions is ₱19,500.

4. APPLICATION [Authentic Application] [Mastery Check]

A worker has basic earnings of ₱20,000, overtime earnings of ₱1,200, an allowance of ₱800, and a benefit of ₱700 included in the problem. The worker has deductions of ₱1,000 and ₱750.

  1. Determine the gross income.
  2. Determine total deductions.
  3. Determine net income.
  4. Explain the difference between gross and net income.
  5. State one reason why checking the classification of each payroll item is important.

Mastery evidence: Learners accurately classify, compute, and explain the payroll information without relying on a fully worked example.

LEARNER GUIDE

Salaries, Wages, Benefits, and Deductions

A four-session study guide for Week 1.

What You Will Learn

  • How salary and wage information is expressed.
  • How to convert an annual salary into monthly and weekly equivalents.
  • How to calculate regular and overtime earnings.
  • How to calculate commission and piecework earnings.
  • How to work with benefits, allowances, and deductions in simplified payroll situations.
  • How to calculate and interpret gross and net income.

Essential Definitions

Term Meaning
Salary A stated amount of pay for a specified period, commonly expressed annually or monthly.
Wage Pay commonly expressed according to a time basis such as an hour or day, or according to work completed.
Allowance An additional amount provided for a specified purpose or condition, according to the stated employment arrangement.
Commission Earnings calculated from a stated percentage of a sales amount or other specified base.
Piecework Earnings based on a rate for each completed unit of work.
Benefit An employment-related benefit or compensation item. Its payroll treatment depends on the applicable rules and the problem's assumptions.
Deduction An amount subtracted from applicable earnings according to the stated payroll arrangement.
Gross income The total income before the deductions specified by the problem.
Net income The amount remaining after the applicable deductions are subtracted.

Key Ideas and Formulas

Monthly salary = Annual salary ÷ 12
Weekly equivalent = Annual salary ÷ 52
Regular earnings = Hourly rate × Regular hours
Overtime rate = Regular hourly rate × Overtime multiplier
Overtime earnings = Overtime rate × Overtime hours
Commission = Sales × Commission rate
Piecework earnings = Rate per unit × Units completed
Total deductions = Sum of applicable deductions
Net income = Gross income − Total deductions
Always identify the time period, rate, base quantity, and type of payroll item before calculating.

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1 — Annual to Monthly Salary

Annual salary = ₱420,000

Monthly salary = ₱420,000 ÷ 12

Answer: ₱35,000 per month

Example 2 — Annual to Weekly Equivalent

Annual salary = ₱420,000

Weekly equivalent = ₱420,000 ÷ 52

Answer: approximately ₱8,076.92 per week

Example 3 — Regular and Overtime Earnings

Hourly rate = ₱120

Regular hours = 40

Overtime hours = 4

Overtime multiplier = 1.25

Regular earnings = ₱120 × 40 = ₱4,800

Overtime rate = ₱120 × 1.25 = ₱150

Overtime earnings = ₱150 × 4 = ₱600

Total = ₱5,400

Example 4 — Commission

Sales = ₱30,000

Commission = 4%

Commission = ₱30,000 × 0.04

Answer: ₱1,200

Example 5 — Piecework

Rate = ₱25 per item

Items completed = 280

Piecework earnings = ₱25 × 280

Answer: ₱7,000

Example 6 — Gross and Net Income

Basic earnings = ₱20,000

Overtime = ₱1,000

Allowance = ₱500

Deductions = ₱900 + ₱600

Gross income = ₱20,000 + ₱1,000 + ₱500 = ₱21,500

Total deductions = ₱900 + ₱600 = ₱1,500

Net income = ₱21,500 − ₱1,500

Answer: ₱20,000

Common Mistakes and Pitfalls

  • Dividing an annual salary by the wrong number of periods.
  • Forgetting to convert a percentage into decimal form when appropriate.
  • Multiplying the overtime multiplier by the wrong quantity.
  • Using sales as piecework units or units as a commission base.
  • Adding deductions instead of subtracting them from gross income.
  • Assuming every allowance or benefit has the same payroll treatment.
  • Giving a numerical answer without interpreting what the amount represents.

Real-World Connections

Payroll mathematics helps workers understand employment offers, payslips, overtime, commissions, allowances, deductions, and take-home income. Understanding the mathematics can help a person check whether a payroll computation is reasonable and make informed financial decisions.

TEACHER IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

Teacher Notes

Evidence-informed decisions, remediation, enrichment, and reflection.

Expected Learner Difficulties

  • Confusing salary, wage, rate, and total earnings.
  • Forgetting the time period represented by an amount.
  • Incorrectly applying percentage rates.
  • Confusing overtime rate with overtime earnings.
  • Using the wrong base for commission.
  • Confusing piecework rate with total piecework earnings.
  • Adding deductions instead of subtracting them.
  • Failing to distinguish gross income from net income.

Evidence-Informed Remediation

If the learner... Evidence suggests... Teacher response
Cannot identify annual/monthly/hourly information Prerequisite vocabulary or time-period understanding is weak. Use a classification activity and visual timeline of pay periods.
Computes overtime incorrectly Rate and multiplier are being confused. Model: regular rate → overtime rate → overtime earnings.
Computes commission incorrectly The percentage base is unclear. Underline the sales base before calculating the percentage.
Gets gross/net income confused Learner does not recognize addition versus subtraction roles. Use a simple income-to-deduction flow diagram and one worked example.

Suggested Differentiation

Support Use partially completed examples, vocabulary cards, formula guides, and structured computation tables.
Core Use contextualized payroll problems with gradually reduced scaffolding.
Extension Use comparison, reverse problems, error analysis, and payroll decision-making tasks.

Suggested Questioning

  • "What information in the problem tells you the time period?"
  • "What quantity is the rate based on?"
  • "Why did you multiply rather than divide?"
  • "What does this percentage represent?"
  • "What would happen if the number of hours changed?"
  • "How can you check whether your answer is reasonable?"
  • "Where did this amount come from in the payroll?"
  • "Should this amount be added or subtracted? Why?"

Technology Integration

  • Use a spreadsheet to organize payroll information.
  • Allow learners to verify calculations using a calculator after showing their mathematical process.
  • Use the class learning website for asynchronous review and additional practice.
  • Use digital exit tickets when available to rapidly identify misconceptions.
