Learning Competencies

Budget of Work — Week 1: Calculate weekly or monthly wages from an annual salary, wages from an hourly rate, including situations involving overtime and other allowances, and earnings based on commission or piecework.
Budget of Work — Week 1: Apply a percentage increase or decrease in various contexts: determining the impact of inflation on costs and wages over time; calculating percentage mark-ups and discounts; calculating VAT; and calculating profit or loss in both absolute and percentage terms.

Learning Objectives

By the end of the five-session learning sequence, learners will be able to:

  1. calculate weekly and monthly wages from annual salaries and calculate regular and overtime earnings from hourly rates;
  2. determine earnings involving allowances, commission, and piecework;
  3. calculate percentage increases and decreases, including inflation, markups, discounts, and VAT;
  4. determine absolute and percentage profit or loss and explain the reasonableness of answers in practical Philippine contexts.
KSA emphasis: Knowledge of financial terms and formulas; skills in accurate computation and interpretation; responsible application of mathematics to personal, household, employment, and small-business decisions.

Learner Context and Planning Notes

  • Learners are expected to have basic division, multiplication, fractions, decimals, and percentages.
  • Learners should understand that one year contains 12 months and approximately 52 weeks for wage calculations.
  • Learners should recognize common Philippine financial contexts such as salaries, hourly work, transportation allowances, retail prices, VAT, discounts, and small-business profit.
  • Some learners may confuse markup with profit percentage, or discount percentage with the final selling price percentage.
  • Concrete peso-based examples should be used before moving to abstract formulas.
  • Calculators or spreadsheet technology may be used after learners understand the mathematical process.

Five-Session ILAW Learning Experience

Session 1 — Annual Salary to Monthly and Weekly Earnings

Explicit Modeling Formative Check Scaffolding

CONNECT: Present a simple payslip or salary statement. Ask: “If a worker is told that the annual salary is ₱360,000, how much might the worker receive per month?”

EXPLORE: Learners identify the relationship among annual, monthly, and weekly pay.

EXPLAIN: Establish the formulas:

Monthly wage = Annual salary ÷ 12
Weekly wage = Annual salary ÷ 52

GUIDED PRACTICE: Solve ₱360,000 ÷ 12 and ₱360,000 ÷ 52 together. Discuss rounding to two decimal places.

COLLABORATIVE LEARNING: Pairs convert several annual salaries into monthly and weekly amounts.

INDEPENDENT PRACTICE: Learners solve three salary conversion problems independently.

REFLECT: Explain why a weekly amount and monthly amount are not obtained using the same divisor.

Session 2 — Hourly Wages, Overtime and Allowances

Explicit Modeling Formative Check Differentiation

CONNECT: Discuss community occupations commonly paid by the hour such as service work, part-time work, and temporary work.

EXPLAIN:

Regular pay = Hourly rate × Regular hours
Overtime pay = Hourly rate × 1.25 × Overtime hours
Gross earnings = Regular pay + Overtime pay + Allowances

Use peso-based examples and clearly distinguish regular hours, overtime hours, and fixed allowances.

GUIDED PRACTICE: A worker earns ₱120 per hour, works 40 regular hours and 4 overtime hours, and receives a ₱300 allowance.

CHECK: Learners identify which amount is regular pay, which amount is overtime pay, and which amount is an allowance before calculating the total.

Session 3 — Commission and Piecework

Explicit Modeling Scaffolding Formative Check

CONNECT: Introduce sales workers, agents, online sellers, tailors, food producers, and other workers whose earnings can depend on sales or output.

Commission = Sales × Commission rate
Total earnings = Base salary + Commission
Piecework earnings = Number of units × Rate per unit

Explain that commission is based on sales while piecework is based on the quantity produced or completed.

GUIDED EXAMPLE: A sales employee earns a base salary of ₱12,000 and receives 5% commission on ₱80,000 in sales.

  1. Convert 5% to decimal: 0.05.
  2. Calculate commission: ₱80,000 × 0.05 = ₱4,000.
  3. Add base salary: ₱12,000 + ₱4,000 = ₱16,000.

Session 4 — Percentage Increase, Decrease, Markup, Discount and VAT

Explicit Modeling Scaffolding Differentiation

CONNECT: Display a Philippine retail price before and after a sale. Ask learners whether a 20% discount means that the customer pays 20% or 80% of the original price.

Percentage change = (Change ÷ Original amount) × 100%
Increase = Original × Increase rate
New amount after increase = Original + Increase
Discount = Original price × Discount rate
Sale price = Original price − Discount
Markup = Cost × Markup rate
Selling price = Cost + Markup
VAT = VAT-exclusive price × VAT rate

Use Philippine peso scenarios involving groceries, transportation, school supplies, clothing, and small-business products.

Important distinction: A 20% discount means the buyer pays 80% of the original price. A 20% markup means the selling price is 120% of the cost.

Session 5 — Profit/Loss and Integrated Financial Problem Solving

Explicit Modeling Mastery Check Differentiation

CONNECT: Present a small sari-sari store or online-selling situation. Learners determine whether a transaction produced a profit or loss.

Profit = Selling price − Cost
Loss = Cost − Selling price
Profit percentage = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100%
Loss percentage = (Loss ÷ Cost) × 100%

Learners complete a multi-step problem involving cost, markup, discount, and final selling price.

REFLECT: Learners explain how mathematical accuracy supports responsible decisions in employment, shopping, and business.

MASTERY CHECK: Learners complete the assessment after reviewing errors from the practice activity.

What You Will Learn

In this lesson, you will learn how mathematics can be used to understand:

  • annual, monthly, and weekly wages;
  • hourly wages and overtime pay;
  • allowances and gross earnings;
  • commission and piecework;
  • percentage increases and decreases;
  • inflation;
  • markups and discounts;
  • VAT; and
  • profit and loss.

1. Earnings from an Annual Salary

An annual salary is the total salary earned over one year. To estimate a monthly salary, divide the annual salary by 12. To estimate a weekly salary, divide it by 52.

Monthly wage = Annual salary ÷ 12
Weekly wage = Annual salary ÷ 52
Worked Example 1

An employee has an annual salary of ₱360,000. Find the monthly salary.

  1. Use Monthly wage = Annual salary ÷ 12.
  2. Monthly wage = ₱360,000 ÷ 12.
  3. Monthly wage = ₱30,000.
Worked Example 2

Find the weekly wage for an annual salary of ₱360,000.

  1. Use Weekly wage = Annual salary ÷ 52.
  2. Weekly wage = ₱360,000 ÷ 52.
  3. Weekly wage = ₱6,923.0769...
  4. Rounded to two decimal places: ₱6,923.08.

2. Hourly Wages and Overtime

Hourly earnings depend on the hourly rate and the number of hours worked.

Regular pay = Hourly rate × Regular hours
Overtime pay = Hourly rate × 1.25 × Overtime hours
Worked Example 3

A worker earns ₱80 per hour and works 8 regular hours plus 2 overtime hours. Calculate the total pay using a 125% overtime rate.

  1. Regular pay = ₱80 × 8 = ₱640.
  2. Overtime rate = ₱80 × 1.25 = ₱100 per overtime hour.
  3. Overtime pay = ₱100 × 2 = ₱200.
  4. Total pay = ₱640 + ₱200 = ₱840.

3. Allowances and Gross Earnings

An allowance is an additional amount provided for a particular purpose, such as transportation or meals. When an allowance is part of the employee's earnings, it can be added to the computed wages.

Gross earnings = Regular pay + Overtime pay + Allowances
Worked Example 4

A worker earns ₱110 per hour, works 40 regular hours and 6 overtime hours, and receives a ₱300 allowance.

  1. Regular pay = ₱110 × 40 = ₱4,400.
  2. Overtime pay = ₱110 × 1.25 × 6 = ₱825.
  3. Allowance = ₱300.
  4. Gross earnings = ₱4,400 + ₱825 + ₱300.
  5. Gross earnings = ₱5,525.

4. Commission and Piecework

Commission

Commission is commonly based on sales. Convert the percentage to decimal before multiplying.

Commission = Sales × Commission rate
Worked Example 5

Find 3% commission on ₱2,000,000 in sales.

  1. 3% = 0.03.
  2. Commission = ₱2,000,000 × 0.03.
  3. Commission = ₱60,000.

Piecework

In a piecework system, payment depends on the number of units completed.

Piecework earnings = Number of units × Rate per unit
Worked Example 6

A tailor receives ₱120 per shirt and completes 15 shirts.

  1. Earnings = 15 × ₱120.
  2. Earnings = ₱1,800.

5. Percentage Increase and Inflation

A percentage increase tells how much an original amount becomes larger. Inflation can cause the prices of goods and services to increase over time.

Increase = Original amount × Increase rate
New amount = Original amount + Increase
Worked Example 7

A household spends ₱10,000 on a group of goods. Prices increase by 5%. What is the new cost?

  1. Increase = ₱10,000 × 0.05 = ₱500.
  2. New cost = ₱10,000 + ₱500.
  3. New cost = ₱10,500.

6. Markup

A markup is an amount added to the cost of a product to determine its selling price.

Markup = Cost × Markup rate
Selling price = Cost + Markup
Worked Example 8

A school bag costs ₱800 and is marked up by 15%. Find the selling price.

  1. Markup = ₱800 × 0.15 = ₱120.
  2. Selling price = ₱800 + ₱120.
  3. Selling price = ₱920.

7. Discount

A discount reduces the original price.

Discount = Original price × Discount rate
Sale price = Original price − Discount
Worked Example 9

A jacket is priced at ₱1,500 and has a 20% discount.

  1. Discount = ₱1,500 × 0.20 = ₱300.
  2. Sale price = ₱1,500 − ₱300.
  3. Sale price = ₱1,200.

8. VAT

VAT is a consumption tax applied to taxable goods and services. In a mathematical problem, always identify whether the stated price is VAT-exclusive or VAT-inclusive before calculating.

VAT = VAT-exclusive price × VAT rate
VAT-inclusive price = VAT-exclusive price + VAT
Worked Example 10

For a practice problem, a service costs ₱2,000 before VAT and the specified VAT rate is 12%. Find the VAT and total amount.

  1. VAT = ₱2,000 × 0.12 = ₱240.
  2. Total = ₱2,000 + ₱240.
  3. Total = ₱2,240.

9. Profit and Loss

Profit occurs when the selling price is greater than the cost. Loss occurs when the selling price is less than the cost.

Profit = Selling price − Cost
Loss = Cost − Selling price
Profit percentage = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100%
Loss percentage = (Loss ÷ Cost) × 100%
Worked Example 11

A product costs ₱500 and is sold for ₱600. Find the profit and profit percentage.

  1. Profit = ₱600 − ₱500 = ₱100.
  2. Profit percentage = (₱100 ÷ ₱500) × 100%.
  3. Profit percentage = 20%.

Common Mistakes and Pitfalls

  • Dividing annual salary by 10 instead of 12 for monthly pay.
  • Using 12 instead of 52 when estimating weekly pay from an annual salary.
  • Adding the overtime rate to the hourly rate instead of multiplying by the overtime multiplier.
  • Confusing markup with discount.
  • Using the selling price rather than the cost as the denominator for profit percentage when the problem defines profit percentage based on cost.
  • Forgetting to convert percentages such as 15% into 0.15 before multiplication.
  • Rounding too early and creating avoidable errors.
  • Ignoring whether a price is VAT-exclusive or VAT-inclusive.

Teacher Notes

Implementation, remediation, enrichment, and reflection guide.

Common Learner Misconceptions

  • Confusing annual, monthly, weekly, and hourly earnings.
  • Using 12 as the divisor for weekly salary.
  • Calculating overtime using the regular hourly rate without the multiplier.
  • Confusing commission with piecework.
  • Adding a discount rather than subtracting it.
  • Thinking a 20% discount means the buyer pays 20% of the original price.
  • Thinking a 20% markup automatically means a 20% profit after a discount.
  • Calculating profit percentage using an inappropriate base.
  • Rounding intermediate calculations too early.

Suggested Remediation

  1. Begin with concrete peso amounts before introducing formulas.
  2. Use a three-column organizer: Original Amount → Change → Final Amount.
  3. Give learners a formula bank for the first practice round.
  4. Use color or labels to distinguish cost, markup, discount, and selling price.
  5. Ask learners to explain what each numerical answer means rather than accepting computation alone.
  6. Pair learners strategically for guided practice.

Suggested Enrichment

  • Ask learners to create a simple product price tag showing cost, markup, marked price, discount, and final price.
  • Let learners compare two employment compensation packages and justify which is financially better under stated assumptions.
  • Create a simple spreadsheet that automatically calculates markup, discount, and profit.
  • Investigate how changes in prices affect a fixed household budget.
